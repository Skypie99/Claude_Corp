Fifteen AI roles work in parallel. One person decides what ships.

Claude Corp is a governed multi-agent development system built at SkyPi Studio. Specialized Claude Code roles plan, build and review on separate branches under a written Constitution. Review gates check the work. Sky holds the merge.

The system

How a cycle runs

The drawing, unrolled: five stations from a directive to a merge.

  1. 01

    01. Directive

    Sky writes the brief and the acceptance gates a cycle will be judged against. The authority order is fixed: Sky's intent, then the Constitution, then each role's file, then its skills.

    Held by Sky

  2. 02

    02. Routing

    Morgan, the project-manager role, reads the backlog, open branches and QA reports, then routes work to the right lanes in dependency order. Morgan is also the only role allowed to brief Sky.

    Morgan · one voice to Sky

  3. 03

    03. Fifteen branches

    Each role works alone on its own role-prefixed branch, never on main. Before starting, it checks for another role's open branch or an existing fix, and writes a conflict report instead of duplicating the work.

    Design and frontend
    Dani, creative direction · Shamus, features · Alex, accessibility
    Backend and data
    Dana, backend · Peter, performance
    Safety, quality and infrastructure
    Steve, safety · Gary, QA · Rory, DevOps
    Product, research and policy
    Quinn, product · Riley, user research · Jordan, privacy · Casey, community · Will, writing
    Coordination
    Morgan, project management · Orion, recovery

    Role names are role names, not people

  4. 04

    04. Review

    Written review gates call for tests, accessibility, privacy and performance checks before anything is proposed for merge, and a seven-layer design-compile review before a UI change counts as done. Orion, the recovery role, resumes failed work from its last checkpoint and escalates after three attempts instead of looping.

    Tests · accessibility · privacy · design compile

  5. 05

    05. Release

    Merges to main need Sky's sign-off. The written rules allow one narrow exception: a single role may merge and release one project, the Prompt Library, and only when every check on a written list passes. Sky reviews that grant quarterly and can revoke it.

    Held by Sky

Decision rights

Who decides what

What the roles do, what stays with Sky, and where the written rules stop.

Decision rights: each decision, who holds it, what the written rules say, and the basis for the claim
DecisionHeld byWhat the rules sayBasis
Intent and rules Sky Sky writes the Constitution, each role's file, the brief and the acceptance gates a cycle is judged against. Only Sky amends the Constitution; roles may propose changes. Basis: Written rule
Build and investigate The roles Each of the fifteen roles works in its own lane and on its own branch, never on main: planning, building, testing, diagnosing and documenting. Before starting, a role checks for another role's open branch or an existing fix. Basis: Written rule, seen in practice
Review and prepare The roles, routed by Morgan Tests, accessibility, privacy and performance checks come before a change is proposed, and a UI change goes through the seven-layer design-compile review. Work arrives as reviewable diffs, migration files and reports. Only Morgan briefs Sky. Basis: Written policy, manual review
Escalate and authorize Sky Conflicts between roles, privacy-sensitive changes, anything involving credentials and anything irreversible come back to Sky, who reads the escalations and the reports of work not done, not only the successes. A role that is unsure is expected to stop and report, not decide. Basis: Written rule
Merge and release Sky Merging to main is Sky's. The written rules allow one narrow exception: one role may merge and release one project, the Prompt Library, only when every listed check passes, including proof from the built site and a recorded rollback. Sky reviews that grant quarterly and can revoke it. Basis: Written rule
Deploy and production Sky No role deploys, submits to an app store, applies a live database change or sends anything outside the repository on its own. The exception above covers that one project's release, and nothing else. Basis: Written rule
Unattended work The roles, on stricter rules Scheduled cycles may make at most one reversible change each, send nothing outside the repository, and stay audit-only on the products by default. A single sentinel file halts all of it. Basis: Written rule
Enforcement Prompt-level These rules are instructions the roles follow, not permissions a platform enforces. Where a project has automated checks, they belong to that project and are verified there. Basis: Known limit

Credibility and limits

Why it can be trusted, and how far

  • Written rules

    A written Constitution and fifteen role files set the authority order and the boundaries above. They are private; this page is a summary.

  • In the source

    The shipped projects' code, tests and commit histories are public, and each project's automated checks live in its own repository.

  • Seen in practice

    Roles write conflict reports instead of duplicating work. Reconciliation, not trust, once caught a session that confidently reported a push to a main that was still behind.

  • Human approval

    Merges, releases, escalations and anything irreversible wait for Sky, apart from the one written exception for the Prompt Library.

The honest limit: these fences are prompt-level, not sandbox-level.

  • The Constitution and role files are private. The public summary is a summary.
  • Claude Corp's own gates are written policy and manual review, not continuous-integration enforcement.
  • The roles are not people and are not always running. They work when Sky invokes them, or on bounded scheduled cycles.
  • Anything that depends on a real device, Safari or a screen reader still needs a person to check it.
  • The drawings on this page explain the operating model. They are not captures of live activity.
  • In use on Sky's own projects only. Some of the overhead does not pay for itself.
